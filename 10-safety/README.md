# 10 - Safety

Safety practices for a home-built, single-seat, 420cc live-axle off-road kart driven by a ~250 lb
(113 kg) adult. Nothing here is a substitute for a sanctioning body's tech inspection; the rulebooks
linked at the bottom are the best free design references for guards, bumpers and pedal stops.

> **Heavy-driver note:** kart + driver will be roughly 400-450 lb all-up. Every energy-related item
> (brakes, bump stops, bumpers, seat mounts, tire ratings) must be sized for that, not for the
> 150 lb driver most plans and kit instructions assume.

---

## 1. Kill switch and tether

- Fit **two** independent ways to kill the engine: a handlebar/dash toggle wired to the magneto kill lead, and a **tether (lanyard) switch** clipped to the driver's wrist or belt that grounds the ignition when pulled.
- Mount the tether switch where a flailing arm cannot pull it accidentally but a thrown driver always will (dash, driver's right side).
- Test both before every ride by starting the engine and killing it with each switch in turn.
- A throttle-return spring failure is the most common runaway cause; use two return springs, one at the carb and one at the pedal.

## 2. Chain, belt and sprocket guards

- Guard the full run of chain and both sprockets on the top and outboard side, and the torque-converter belt on the top and outboard side. Sheet steel 16-18 ga or 1/8" aluminum, bolted (not just tacked) so it can be removed for service.
- Nothing from the driver's body or clothing may reach a chain, belt or sprocket while seated. Check with the seat installed and a helper wiggling a glove near the drive.
- CIK-FIA and WKA both require a chain guard that covers the sprocket and chain top run; copy that geometry.

## 3. Hot exhaust

- Route the muffler away from the seat, fuel tank and the driver's calves. Add a perforated heat shield standoff 3/8"-1/2" off the muffler if any part is within reach of a hand or leg.
- Keep fuel line, throttle cable and wiring at least 2" from the exhaust and sleeve them where they cross.
- Grass and leaves pack in behind a muffler on an off-road kart and ignite; leave an open gap below it.

## 4. Fuel system

- Steel or HDPE tank with a vented cap, mounted **behind or beside** the driver, never above the engine or ahead of the pedals.
- Inline shutoff valve, inline filter, SAE 30R7 fuel line with spring clamps. Replace cracked line immediately.
- No gravity-feed tank mounted above an engine on a kart that can roll; a rollover turns it into a fuel shower on a hot muffler.
- Fill outdoors, engine off and cool. Never store a kart with fuel inside a living space.

## 5. Brake redundancy

- Minimum: a hydraulic or mechanical disc brake on the live axle sized for the all-up weight (6-8" rotor, 1" axle). Band brakes are not adequate at 400+ lb.
- Preferred: hydraulic disc on the axle plus a mechanical cable to the same or a second caliper, or a second pedal-actuated caliper. If one system fails the other still stops the kart.
- Pedal must have a positive stop so it cannot over-travel and bend the master cylinder pushrod.
- Bleed hydraulic systems on a stand and test with the rear wheels off the ground before the first ride.

## 6. Seat belt and harness: both views

**Case for a belt (with a roll cage):** on a kart with a real roll cage, a 4- or 5-point harness keeps the driver inside the protected volume in a rollover; sanctioned off-road and UTV classes require it. If you build a cage, you must belt in.

**Case against a belt (no cage):** on an open sprint-style kart with no cage, racing bodies (WKA, IKF, CIK-FIA) **prohibit** belts. In a rollover you want to be thrown clear rather than pinned under a 200 lb machine with a hot exhaust. The kart is low and the driver's body is the "cage."

**The rule of thumb:** no cage, no belt; cage and belt together, or neither. A lap belt alone on an open kart is the worst of both: it holds you under the kart without protecting you.

For an off-road yard kart that will see slopes and ruts, a cage with a harness is the safer design; see section 7.

## 7. Roll bar and cage for off-road use

- A single hoop behind the seat is a minimum for off-road use. Top of the hoop at least 3" above the helmet with the driver seated.
- Tube: 1-1/4" x 0.120" wall DOM minimum for a hoop carrying a 250 lb driver; 1" x 0.095" is too light. Gusset the hoop to the frame rails with triangulated braces, never a single weld at the base.
- Add a diagonal brace in the hoop and two rear braces to the frame at 30-45 degrees.
- Pad any tube within reach of the helmet with roll-bar padding, not pipe insulation.
- Harness mounts must land on the frame or a cross tube, with 1/4" or thicker weld plates, never on sheet metal or the seat shell.

## 8. Helmet and gear

- DOT or ECE 22.06 full-face helmet; Snell M/SA or CIK-FIA-approved for competition. Replace after any impact.
- Eye protection (visor or goggles), gloves, long sleeves and pants, over-the-ankle boots. Neck brace (foam collar) is required by youth karting bodies and is cheap insurance.
- No loose clothing, scarves or hoodie strings near the drive side.

## 9. Steering stops and bump stops

- Weld or bolt a positive stop so the spindle cannot swing far enough to let the tire touch the frame, the tie rod cross over center, or the steering shaft bind.
- Use a stop on the frame against the spindle arm, not the tie-rod end as a stop.
- Stops must have a 1/4" or larger steel contact face; a bolt head is fine.

## 10. Wheel nuts, cotter pins and safety wire

- Spindles: castle nut with a cotter pin, or a nyloc nut plus a cotter pin through a drilled spindle. Never a plain nut.
- Live-axle hubs: set screws over the key, plus a drilled set screw with safety wire or a jam set screw. Mark the hub position with paint and check for walk after every ride.
- Lug nuts on 4-bolt hubs: torque and paint-mark; re-check after the first 3 rides.
- Pedal pivots, kingpins, tie-rod ends and brake levers all get a cotter pin, safety wire or nyloc.

## 11. Fire extinguisher

- A 2.5 lb ABC dry-chemical extinguisher in a quick-release bracket on the kart or on the trailer, and another in the shop within 10 ft of the welding area.
- Shake it monthly; dry-chem packs down and will not discharge.

## 12. Torque spec sheet: common fasteners

Values are for **clean, dry, zinc-plated** SAE bolts; reduce about 25% if lubricated or anti-seized. Source charts: Bolt Depot grade chart (https://boltdepot.com/fastener-information/Materials-and-Grades/Bolt-Grade-Chart) and the AERA/Melling bolt-marking bulletin (https://melling.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/Common-Bolt-Markings-Torques-Tech-Bulletin-AERA-Melling-Engine-Parts.pdf).

| Size | Grade 5 (3 radial lines) | Grade 8 (6 radial lines) | Typical kart use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1/4"-20 | 8 ft-lb | 12 ft-lb | Guards, pedal brackets |
| 5/16"-18 | 17 ft-lb | 24 ft-lb | Engine mount, seat, brake caliper |
| 3/8"-16 | 30 ft-lb | 45 ft-lb | Engine mount, bearing flanges, hub lugs |
| 7/16"-14 | 50 ft-lb | 70 ft-lb | Spindle bolts, steering shaft |
| 1/2"-13 | 75 ft-lb | 105 ft-lb | Kingpin bolts, roll hoop plates |
| 5/8"-11 | 150 ft-lb | 210 ft-lb | Spindle axle nuts (back off to the cotter pin) |

- Use Grade 5 for everything except kingpins, spindle bolts, engine mount, axle sprocket/brake hubs and cage plates, which get Grade 8.
- Never use Grade 2 (no head marks) or unknown stainless hardware on load-bearing joints.
- Thin nuts and wing nuts are not fasteners for a kart.

## 13. Weld inspection basics

- **Look** for consistent bead width, full wrap around the tube joint, no undercut at the toe, no cold lap (weld sitting on top of base metal), and no crater cracks at the stop.
- **Fish-mouth** every tube joint so there is no gap over 1/16" before welding; large gaps bridged with filler are the first thing to crack.
- **Test coupons:** before the frame, weld two scraps of the same tube at 90 degrees and beat the joint with a 3 lb hammer until it fails. The tube should tear beside the weld, not the weld itself.
- **Penetration check:** cut and etch a test joint; a bead that has not fused the root of the joint is a tack, not a weld.
- Weld in a cross pattern (alternate sides) to limit warping; measure wheelbase and track diagonally after welding and before adding anything bolt-on.
- MIG with 0.030" ER70S-6 wire and 75/25 gas on 0.083"-0.120" tube is the normal hobby setup. The Fabricator's Q&A with Red Beard's Garage covers the practical side of kart welding: https://www.thefabricator.com/thewelder/article/arcwelding/a-qa-with-the-welder-behind-popular-youtube-channel-red-beards-garage

## 14. First-ride checklist

1. Kart on a stand, rear wheels off the ground; start engine, check idle (wheels must not creep), kill with each switch.
2. Throttle snaps closed on release; two return springs.
3. Brake holds the rear wheels against full throttle on the stand.
4. All wheel nuts and axle set screws torqued and paint-marked; cotter pins in every castle nut.
5. Steering lock-to-lock with no binding, bump stops hit before the tire touches anything.
6. Chain tension 1/2"-3/4" slack, sprockets aligned with a straightedge.
7. Guards on, exhaust shield on, no fuel smell, shutoff valve open.
8. Flat, open, dry area; no spectators within 50 ft; helmet and gear on.
9. First pass at walking speed, brake test, then increase in steps. Stop, re-check every fastener after 10 minutes.
10. Inspect welds for cracks after the first full session.

## 15. Pre-ride inspection (every ride)

- Tires: pressure, no cuts, beads seated.
- Wheel nuts and hub set screws: tap with a wrench, look at the paint marks.
- Steering: free play under 1" at the wheel rim; tie-rod ends tight.
- Brake: pedal firm, no fluid at fittings, pads above 2 mm.
- Chain: tension and lube; look for stiff links.
- Throttle: returns fully; cable not frayed.
- Fuel: level, no leaks, cap vented, shutoff open.
- Kill switch and tether: tested.
- Frame and cage: quick look at every weld near the seat, engine mount and rear axle.
- Guards, seat mounts, harness (if caged): bolted and unbroken.

## 16. Liability and legal note

- A home-built kart is **not street legal** in any US state and cannot be registered or insured for road use; it has no lights, no DOT tires, no VIN. Riding on public roads, sidewalks or shoulders is a citation at best.
- Ride only on your own land or with the written permission of the landowner. Many homeowner policies exclude motorized recreational vehicles; ask your insurer before letting anyone else drive it.
- Minors and guests: you are liable for anyone you let ride. Require a helmet and a walk-through of the controls.
- Local noise ordinances apply; a 420cc with a stock muffler is around 90 dB at the seat.
- Keep a written build log with photos of welds; it helps if you ever sell the kart and it matters to an insurer.
