# 09 - Parts Sourcing

Vendor directory and a sample bill of materials for a single-seat, 420cc, live-axle off-road kart
built for a ~250 lb (113 kg) driver. Links were checked against search-engine indexes in November 2026;
anything that could not be confirmed is named without a link. Prices are estimates, dated late 2026,
US dollars, before shipping and tax. Check the live page before ordering.

> **Heavy-driver note:** most "fun kart" kits, kit frames, and plans assume a 150-200 lb driver and
> often print a 150-180 lb or 250 lb gross limit. At 250 lb driver weight you are at or over the limit
> of almost every bolt-together kit and many stamped-steel components. Where it matters below it is called out.

## Full kit vs piece-by-piece

- **Bolt-together kits** (e.g. the Bolt Go-Kart Kit reviewed at https://silodrome.com/bolt-together-go-kart-kit/, ~$1,600 without engine) need no welding but are built around a ~150-200 lb driver and a 212cc engine. Not a good fit at 250 lb with a 420cc.
- **Parts kits for a plan** (GoPowerSports KartFab kit, ~$650-700) save a lot of searching but include 3/4" live-axle parts, single-wheel drive and a stamped seat. Upgrade the axle, hubs, bearings and seat if you use one.
- **Piece-by-piece** costs about the same once you upgrade, and lets you buy a 1" or 1-1/4" axle, 4-bolt hubs, a hydraulic disc brake and a proper seat from the start. That is the recommended route for this build.

## Heavy-driver upgrades worth the money

- 1-1/4" axle with three bearings instead of 1" with two: roughly $60 more, removes the most common failure (axle flex and bearing walkout).
- 4-bolt hubs instead of 3-bolt: ~$30 more per pair.
- Hydraulic brake instead of band brake: ~$100 more; band brakes fade badly with 400+ lb all-up weight.
- 4-ply or 6-ply rear tires: ~$20 per tire; 2-ply turf tires squirm and roll off the bead under a heavy driver in corners.
- Gear 1-2 teeth lower than plan recommendations on the driver or 6-10 teeth higher on the axle; the torque converter will spend less time slipping.

## Ordering tips

- Buy the engine, torque converter, axle and hubs first and measure them before cutting tubing; crank height and axle diameter drive the frame layout.
- Match bores before checkout: engine crank (1" on 420cc), driver bore, driven bore (usually 1" jackshaft or 3/4"), axle sprocket hub bore.
- Buy bearings and sprockets in pairs and keep a spare chain; freight is the expensive part of a second order.
- Avoid "universal" spindle kits with no stated shaft diameter or steel grade. A 5/8" spindle with a 1/2" kingpin bolt is the minimum at this driver weight.
- Keep every invoice and part number in a build log; it makes reordering a bent spindle or a lost key a five-minute job.
