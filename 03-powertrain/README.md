# 03 – Powertrain (engine, clutch/torque converter, gearing, exhaust, throttle)

## Heavy driver notes (250 lb / 113 kg driver, ~400–450 lb all-up)
- Most "6.5 hp kart" advice assumes a 150 lb driver and ~300 lb all-up. You are carrying roughly 40–50% more mass, so acceleration, hill climbing and clutch heat all get worse in proportion.
- A stock Predator 212 (6.5 hp gross, ~8 ft-lb) will move you on flat ground but is **marginal on grass hills**. A Predator 420 (13 hp, ~18 ft-lb) or a built 212/224 (governor out, stage-1 intake/exhaust, billet flywheel) is the comfortable choice.
- Use a **torque converter (CVT), not a centrifugal clutch**. A clutch on 450 lb slips long and hot at every launch and on every hill; a torque converter drops to ~2.7:1 low range automatically and keeps the engine in its torque band.
- Gear lower than the "typical" charts: aim for 30 mph, not 40. Lower gearing is what saves the belt, clutch and engine on hills.
- Use #40/#420 chain and a 1" or larger engine-side hardware stack on a 420; #35 is a racing-kart chain and is the first thing to break on a heavy yard kart.

Why the 212 is marginal for you (rough physics, estimates):
- Climbing a 15° (27%) grass slope: grade force = 450 lb x sin 15° ≈ 116 lb, plus ~5% rolling resistance on grass (~22 lb) ≈ **140 lb of tractive force**.
- On a 20" tire (10" radius) that is 1,400 in-lb = **~117 ft-lb at the axle**.
- A 212 makes ~8 ft-lb. Through a 30-series CVT in low (2.7:1) and 7.2:1 chain, with ~85% drivetrain efficiency: 8 x 2.7 x 7.2 x 0.85 ≈ **132 ft-lb** – just enough, and only in low range at walking pace.
- A 420 (~18 ft-lb) gives ~110 ft-lb **in high range** and ~300 ft-lb in low. It climbs without thinking about it.
- Power check: 140 lb x 10 mph (14.7 ft/s) = 2,060 ft-lb/s ≈ 3.7 hp at the wheels, which is most of a stock 212's net output. The 420 does it at a third throttle.

## Clutch vs torque converter
- **Centrifugal clutch** (~$30–60): engages ~1,800–2,000 rpm, fixed ratio. Cheap and light but it slips until the kart reaches lock-up speed. With 450 lb and hills it will glaze and blue. Only acceptable for a kid-weight kart on flat pavement.
- **Torque converter / CVT** (~$100–250): variable ratio from ~2.7:1 (launch) to ~0.9–1:1 (cruise). The engine stays near peak torque while the belt does the ratio change. For a heavy driver this is the single biggest drivability upgrade and it protects the engine from lugging.
- **Comet/TAV2 30 series** (3/4" bore driver): rated up to ~8 hp, 2–7 hp per some vendors. Correct for 212/224/GX200. Standard 10T #40/#420 or 12T #35 output sprocket. Predator cranks sit ~1/8" lower than Honda; use the supplied crank washer/spacer.
- **Comet 40 / 44 series** (1" bore driver): 8–18 hp, the correct choice for a 420/GX390/459. Wider belt, larger driven pulley (7-1/2" on 40, 44 uses the Magnum driver and a symmetric belt). Do not try to put a 40 driver on a 30-series backplate (belt center distance differs).
- Belt life: a 30-series belt on a 212 pulling 450 lb up hills is the wear item; keep a spare and keep gearing low so the belt isn't forced to stay in low range.
- Asian clones of the TAV2 are everywhere and mostly fine; buy a genuine Comet belt even if the unit is a clone.

## Chain, sprockets, tensioners, sprocket hubs

| Chain | Pitch | Avg tensile (approx) | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| #35 | 3/8" | ~2,100 lb | Racing karts, kids karts, light minibikes. Not for a 450 lb off-road kart. |
| #41 | 1/2" (narrow) | ~2,000 lb (estimate) | Cheap yard-kart chain; fits #40/#420 sprockets. Acceptable on a 212, weakest of the 1/2" family. |
| #40 | 1/2" | ~3,700 lb (ANSI) | Industrial roller chain, same sprockets as #41/#420. Good choice. |
| #420 | 1/2" (taller plates) | ~3,700 lb | Motorcycle chain; strongest common kart chain. **Recommended for 212+ with a heavy driver, mandatory on a 420.** |

- Sprocket hubs: 1" bore split (clamp) hub with 1/4" key, 4-bolt or 6-bolt sprocket pattern. Buy a hub + bolt-on sprocket rather than a one-piece hub/sprocket so you can re-gear. For 1-1/4" axle use a 1-1/4" bore hub.
- Chain tensioner: a spring-loaded idler on the slack side, or a slotted engine plate. On a torque converter kit the engine plate is slotted; set 1/4"–1/2" of chain deflection, re-check after the first hour (new chain stretches).
- Alignment: sprocket and clutch must be in the same plane within ~1/32". Misalignment is the usual cause of thrown chains on live-axle karts.
- Master links: use a clip-type master with the closed end facing the direction of travel; carry a spare.

## Gear ratio math (worked example for 250 lb driver)
Top speed (mph) = engine rpm x tire diameter (in) x π x 60 / (overall ratio x 63,360)

Rearranged: required overall ratio = rpm x dia x π x 60 / (mph x 63,360)

Example: Predator 212 or 420, governed 3,600 rpm, torque converter in high (treat as 1:1), 20" tires, target 30 mph:
- ratio = 3,600 x 20 x 3.1416 x 60 / (30 x 63,360) = 13,571,680 / 1,900,800 ≈ **7.1:1**
- Nearest real sprockets: 10T driver / 72T axle = 7.2:1 → 29.8 mph at 3,600 rpm.
- Launch ratio in CVT low: 2.7 x 7.2 ≈ 19.4:1 – that is what makes hills work.

Quick table (3,600 rpm, CVT in high ≈ 1:1):

| Tire | 10/60 (6.0:1) | 10/66 (6.6:1) | 10/72 (7.2:1) | 12/72 (6.0:1, #35 only) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 18" | 32 mph | 29 mph | 27 mph | 32 mph |
| 20" | 36 mph | 32 mph | 30 mph | 36 mph |
| 22" | 39 mph | 36 mph | 33 mph | 39 mph |

Heavy-driver guidance: pick the column that gives 28–32 mph, not 36–39. On a 212 with 20–22" tires that means 10/72 or a jackshaft. On a 420 you can run 10/60–10/66 and still have hill torque to spare. If you remove the governor and spin 5,000 rpm, re-run the math – 10/72 on 20" becomes 41 mph, which is fast for a yard kart with 6" rotors.

## Jackshafts
- Needed when the ratio you want can't be reached in one chain stage (axle sprocket would exceed ~72T or hit the ground) or when the engine can't sit beside the axle.
- Typical: 3/4" keyed jackshaft in two 3/4" bearings, 10T in / 20T out on stage one, then 10T / 60T to the axle = 12:1 overall. Lets a 212 pull a heavy kart on 22" tires without a dinner-plate sprocket.
- Cost: shaft $20–25, bearings + hangers $25–40, sprockets $10–20 each, or a kit ~$60.

## Exhaust, air filter, throttle
- Stock muffler is fine and quiet; a "stage 1" header plus open filter is worth ~1–1.5 hp on a 212 only after governor removal and a re-jet (main jet ~.038–.040 on a non-Hemi, vendor kits include jets). On a 420 leave the stock exhaust unless you want noise.
- Air filter: if you ride dust, keep a foam pre-filter over the open-element filter; a paper element in a stock box is better for dirt than a bare cone.
- Throttle: use a kart throttle cable (~5–6 ft, 1/16" inner wire) with a cable-clamp bracket at the carb, a return spring at the pedal AND the carb (two springs, so a broken one can't leave you at full throttle), and a pedal stop so you can't over-travel the carb linkage. Route the cable with no bends tighter than ~4" radius.
- Kill switch within reach of the driver's hand, wired to the engine's stop lead; test it before every ride.

## Governor removal, rpm limits, billet flywheel
- Stock 212/224/420 governors hold ~3,600 rpm. Removing the governor (gear and arm out, holes plugged) lets the engine reach 5,000–6,000+ rpm and typically adds 1.5–2.5 hp on a 212 with intake/exhaust mods.
- **Risk:** the stock cast flywheel is not designed for sustained high rpm; the widely repeated community rule is "no governor = billet flywheel" and that cast flywheels become a hazard somewhere above ~5,000 rpm (community consensus, not a published failure test). A flywheel failure at 6,000 rpm sends shrapnel through the shroud toward your legs.
- Stock valve springs float around ~5,200 rpm; a de-governed engine needs 18–26 lb springs or it will drop a valve.
- Billet flywheels (ARC and others) are ~$100–140 and are SFI rated. Many are also lighter, which costs a little low-end flywheel effect but makes little practical difference on a heavy kart with a CVT.
- For your weight the honest advice: buy a 420 and leave the governor in, or de-govern a 212 only as a full package (billet flywheel, springs, header, re-jet, chromoly pushrods).
