# 06 – Wheels, tires, live axle, seat and harness

## Heavy driver notes (250 lb driver, ~400–450 lb all-up)
- Rear axle carries ~60% of the kart: ~270 lb static, and 2–3x that in a landing or a rut (500–800 lb est.). A 1" cold-rolled axle at 40"+ between bearings will visibly bow with you in the seat.
- **Recommended: 1-1/4" keyed axle** (1045 or chromoly tube), with bearing hangers close to the wheel hubs. **1" is the minimum** and only with a short unsupported span (bearings within ~6" of each hub, so ≤ ~24" between bearings).
- Tires: combined load rating of all four tires should be **≥ 2x gross weight ≈ 900 lb**, and each rear tire alone ≥ ~300 lb at your running pressure. Cheap 2-ply 145/70-6 tires (99–165 lb each) do not qualify.
- Seats: most "go kart seats" are 15–16" between the hip bolsters. At 250 lb you need **18–20" inside hip width** – XL kart seats, UTV/buggy buckets or suspension seats. Mount the seat to a cross-braced sub-frame, not to a single tube.
- Front wheels: use 5/8" (or 3/4") bore wheels with **two sealed ball bearings per wheel**, not bronze bushings.

## Live axle sizing
| Axle | Material | Span between bearings | Rating for 450 lb off-road | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 3/4" | 1018 CRS | any | Not suitable | Minibike / kid kart only |
| 1" solid | 1018/1045 CRS, 1/4" keyway | ≤ 24" | Minimum | Bends at long spans; keyway cuts ~15% from stiffness; use 1045 if offered |
| 1-1/4" solid or 0.190" wall tube | 1045 or 42CrMo chromoly | up to ~36" | **Recommended** | ~2.4x the bending stiffness of 1"; racing-kart standard, lots of hubs/bearings/rotors available |
| 1-1/2" | tube | any | Overkill | Buggy/UTV territory, few kart parts |

- Stiffness goes with diameter⁴: 1-1/4" vs 1" = (1.25)⁴ = 2.44x. That is the whole argument.
- Keyways: 1" axles use 1/4" keys; 1-1/4" use 1/4" (American) or 8 mm (European racing) – buy hubs to match. Full-length keyways are convenient but weaken the shaft; stepped/partial keyways are stronger.
- Length: track width + hub widths + ~1" each side. Typical 40–44" axle for a 36–38" track with 8" wheels.
- Set screws on hubs/sprocket hubs should land in a drilled dimple in the axle (not just on the key) and get blue thread locker. Snap-ring grooves (racing axles) are the cleaner way to locate hubs.

## Axle bearings and hangers
- Bearing type: sealed radial ball bearings in **3-bolt flange** (most common, 1" and 1-1/4"), **2-bolt flange** (lighter, 1" only), or **4-bolt flange** (heavier, often 1-1/4"+ and pillow-block style). For your weight use 3-bolt or 4-bolt; the 2-bolt style twists on a bent frame.
- Flangette pairs: the bearing is sandwiched between two stamped flanges bolted through a weld-on hanger. Use 5/16" Grade 5 bolts minimum; hangers of 3/16" plate, welded on both sides.
- Pillow blocks (cast housing, self-aligning insert bearing, set-screw lock): tolerate frame misalignment, bolt to a flat plate, cost $10–20 each. Good choice for a home-built frame that isn't perfectly square. Use 1" or 1-1/4" bore, UCP205-16 (1") / UCP206-20 (1-1/4") are the generic numbers.
- Three hangers (one in the middle) on a 1" axle is a common bodge; it works only if all three are dead in line, otherwise it binds. Better to go 1-1/4" with two.
- Self-aligning (spherical OD) inserts forgive ~2° of misalignment; standard flange bearings forgive almost none – shim hangers until the axle spins freely by hand with both wheels on.
- Rebuild interval: cheap sealed bearings last 1–3 seasons in dirt; grease-able pillow blocks last longer.

## Hubs and keyways
- Rear wheel hubs: keyed bore (1" with 1/4" key, 1-1/4" with 1/4" or 8 mm), one or two set screws, bolt flange for the wheel. Aluminum is fine; steel for abuse.
- Wheel bolt patterns (kart): **4 on 4"** (American 8" wheels, Manco/Yerf-Dog, most off-road kart wheels), **3 on 2-1/2"** (Azusa/racing 5"–6" wheels), **3 on 58 mm** (European racing). Decide the wheel first, then buy hubs.
- Sprocket/brake hubs use the same bore/key as wheel hubs: 4-bolt (racing: 6-bolt) flange on a 3"–4" circle.
- 1-1/4" 4-on-4" hubs are rarer than 1" ones; sources below carry them. Alternatively run 3-on-2-1/2" aluminum racing hubs with 6" or 8" racing wheels.

## Wheels
| Rim | Typical tire | Pattern | Use |
|---|---|---|---|
| 5" | 11x4.00-5, 11x6.00-5 (racing slicks) | 3-on-2-1/2" | Pavement/racing; too small for grass with your weight |
| 6" | 145/70-6, 13x5.00-6, 15x6.00-6 | 3-on-2-1/2" or 4-on-4" | Common yard-kart size; OK for front wheels |
| 8" | 16x6.50-8, 18x8.50-8, 18x9.50-8, 19x7-8, 20x10-8, 22x11-8 | 4-on-4" | **Recommended rear (and front) for off-road at 450 lb**: more load rating, more diameter over ruts |

- Steel rims take dents and keep sealing; aluminum racing rims are lighter and pricier. Two-piece split rims make tire changes easy.
- **Tubeless vs tube**: tubeless 8" lawn/ATV tires on a good rim hold 4–8 psi well and resist pinch flats. 6" kart tires on cheap rims often weep; run tubes if they won't seal. Carry a plug kit either way.
- Front wheels: 6" or 8" with **5/8" ID sealed bearings (R10-2RS / 1630-2RS style, two per wheel)** and a center spacer tube so the axle nut can be torqued without side-loading the bearings. 3/4" bearing wheels if you chose 3/4" spindles.

## Tire choices and load ratings
- Load ratings are printed on the sidewall ("MAX LOAD xxx LBS AT yy PSI"). Compare at the same pressure. Ply rating (2/4/6 PR) is the quick proxy.
- Requirement for this kart: rear tires ≥ 300 lb each at running pressure, front ≥ 200 lb each, **total ≥ 900 lb** (2x gross for bumps). Examples (vendor-published, approx):

| Tire | Ply | Max load | Verdict |
|---|---|---|---|
| 145/70-6 (cheap) | 2 PR | ~99–165 lb @ 5 psi | No – rear would be at 2x rating |
| 145/70-6 | 4–6 PR | ~165–265 lb | Front only, marginal |
| 18x9.50-8 Turf Saver | 2 PR | 695 lb @ 12 psi | Yes |
| 18x9.50-8 Turf Saver | 4 PR | 935 lb @ 20 psi | Yes, with margin |
| 18x9.50-8 Turf Saver | 6 PR | 1,295 lb @ 36 psi | Yes (stiff ride) |
| 16x6.50-8 / 18x8.50-8 turf | 2–4 PR | roughly 400–800 lb (check sidewall) | Yes for fronts and rears |
| 20x10-8 / 22x11-8 ATV knobby | 2–6 PR | typically 300–600+ lb | Yes; best grip in mud; tall – re-gear |

- Tread: turf/"sawtooth" for lawn (low damage, decent traction), ATV knobby for mud and loose dirt, ribbed fronts for steering bite on grass.
- Tire pressure: 8" turf/ATV tires **5–8 psi** off-road (more sidewall flex = more grip and a softer ride), 10–12 psi on pavement; never exceed the sidewall max. With your weight run the upper end of the range in the rear (7–8 psi) so the bead stays seated in turns. 6" kart tires: 10–15 psi.
- Matched pairs: same diameter left/right on a live axle or the kart will crab.

## Seats
- Measure yourself: sit on a flat bench and measure hip width at the widest point; add 1–2" for clothing. Most 250 lb adults need **18–20" inside width** and a seat back at least 20" tall for shoulder support.
- Options:
  - **Plastic "jumbo"/XL kart seats** (injected plastic, ~19" inside, 15" waist) – cheap ($50–90), rigid, no padding; add a 1" closed-cell pad.
  - **Fiberglass racing kart seats, size XL/XXL** (Tillett T8/T11 XL, OTK "XL") – strong, shaped, $150–300; sized by a cm scale, ask for 36–38 cm+ internal hip width.
  - **UTV / buggy bucket seats** (universal buggy bucket on rails, RZR-style take-offs, 20–21" wide) – most comfortable, upholstered, heavy (15–25 lb), $80–250; need 4 mounting points on a flat sub-frame.
  - **Suspension seats** (PRP GT/S.E. 1" extra-wide, RST; Pro Armor LE) – fabric suspended in a tube frame, absorb bumps on an unsuspended kart, $250–700 each. The best choice for a heavy driver on a rigid off-road kart if the budget allows.
- Mounting: build a seat sub-frame of 1" square tube (or 1" x 1/8" angle) spanning **two** frame cross members, with the seat bolted through at four points with 5/16" Grade 5 bolts and large washers. Never hang a seat off one tube by two tabs – a 250 lb driver bouncing cracks the weld at the tab root. Put a 1" tube behind the seat back and brace it to the rear hoop/engine mount.
- Isolate rigid seats with 1/4" rubber pads between seat and sub-frame; it stops cracking in plastic seats and takes the ring out of fiberglass.
- Seat position: slide forward until your knee has ~30° bend at full pedal travel; a cramped seat is why heavy drivers lift off the pedals in bumps.

## Harness / seat belt basics
- A yard kart with a roll hoop needs a belt; a kart without a roll hoop should **not** have a belt (you want to be thrown clear, not held under a flipped kart). Decide the roll hoop first.
- With a hoop: 4-point harness (2" or 3" lap, 2" shoulders) with a push-button or cam-lock buckle. Lap belt anchors on the frame **behind the hip point, angled 45–60° down** so the belt pulls down over the pelvis, not up into the gut. Shoulder straps anchor to the hoop cross bar **within 0–20° below shoulder height**; steeper down-angles compress the spine in a frontal hit.
- Anchors: weld 1/4" plate tabs to the frame tubes (not to the seat) with 7/16" Grade 8 eye bolts/clips; back them with the tube, never a sheet-metal floor. Each lap anchor should hold ~3,000 lb (the belt's rating), i.e. ~10x your weight.
- Belt length: standard 4-point harnesses fit to ~50" waist; check the lap belt max length before buying at 250 lb.
- No belt + no hoop = ride speed stays sane and wear a helmet. A belt on a hoop-less kart is the one combination to avoid.
